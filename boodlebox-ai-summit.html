CONTEXT

Project Background & The AI Landscape

• The College Context: As a small liberal arts college, Bryn Mawr is still in the 'figuring things out' stage of adopting AI. No college-wide policy yet, but students and faculty are actively using it.

• Existing AI Touchpoints: AI features had trickled into existing toolsets (Microsoft 365, Zoom, Adobe Creative Cloud), but we lacked a dedicated, pedagogical AI platform.

• The Catalyst: Chris Barth, LITS Chief Information Officer (CIO), established a BoodleBox pilot in late 2025, which expanded into a full institution-wide license.

• The Opportunity: Since BoodleBox is built specifically as an educational technology, the EAST team immediately began exploring hands-on pedagogical use cases.

• The Idea: EAST supervisor Jenny Spohrer proposed integrating BoodleBox into our existing Digital Technology Internship (DTI) program.

PROJECT MOTIVATION

Common Faculty Goals

Deepening Course Engagement

Not all students feel comfortable coming to office hours or asking questions in front of peers. A virtual TA lowers the barrier to seeking help 24/7.

'Saving' Students from Generic Bots

Steering students away from unvetted, hallucination-prone general AI. Instead, provide a bot grounded directly in faculty course materials.

Scaling Review & Feedback

Individual detailed feedback on every practice problem or exam question is difficult to scale. Virtual TAs help fill this critical instructional gap.

Curated Resource Direction

Directing students to vetted professor materials and high-quality public resources (e.g., Khan Academy) when extra help is needed.

BOT ARCHITECTURE

How We Built: Custom Bot Variables

• Model Selection: Which underlying LLM best balances reasoning capability, pedagogical tone, and reliability? (Tested ChatGPT and Claude versions)

• Prompt Instructions & Guardrails: What exact behavioral guidelines ensure the bot engages in Socratic questioning rather than just giving away direct answers?

• Knowledge Bank & Course Materials: What documents should the bot ingest? Should it contain the full semester syllabus/materials or only content covered to date?

• Scope & Boundaries: Should the bot restrict answers solely to course content, or direct students to vetted external resources for broader context?

LESSONS & SOLUTIONS

Challenges Encountered Along the Way

• Direct Answers vs. Socratic Inquiry: Challenge: Should bots give information directly or prompt deeper thinking?
Solution: Socratic questioning by default; bot steps in with direct hints if student is genuinely stuck.

• External Resource Curation: Challenge: What external materials should the bot recommend?
Solution: Settled on high-quality, publicly available resources (e.g., Khan Academy for quantitative prep).

• Model Selection Discretion: Challenge: ChatGPT vs. Claude models behaved differently across disciplines.
Solution: Student interns built dual versions; professors chose their preferred model while archiving the other.

• Gamification & Engagement: Challenge: Keeping students motivated to engage repeatedly.
Solution: Pragya implemented a 'point counter' system tracking progress through modules.

• LMS Integration vs. Linking: Challenge: Embedding tools directly into Moodle course pages.
Solution: Full iframe integration wasn't viable; settled on prominent deep-linking within Moodle courses.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

Lessons Learned & Next Steps

Key Lessons Learned

• Be thorough in writing instructions: Precise prompt phrasing and behavioral boundary definitions are critical for pedagogical bots.

• Course materials make the bot: High-quality, professor-created materials are the single best foundation for domain-specific RAG performance.

• Faculty are essential partners: Professors are irreplaceable for evaluating bot nuances, factual precision, and educational efficacy.

• The bot needs to be the center if it’s to be used: The PHYS 101 bot wasn’t used as much because it was buried within the site.

Next Steps

• Spring Rollout of BIOL 202 TA: Deploying Greg Davis's biology assistant with full semester tracking.

• Expanding Faculty Cohorts: Recruiting additional STEM and Humanities faculty for custom bot pilots.

• Enhanced Assessment & Learning Analytics: Measuring student learning outcomes and engagement patterns across courses.

• DTI Program Continuation: Training future student intern cohorts to lead bot maintenance and prompt engineering.

• Integration: are there ways to integrate this bot with other platforms (e.g., Moodle)? Also, can the bot remain centered if we integrate it into something else.
